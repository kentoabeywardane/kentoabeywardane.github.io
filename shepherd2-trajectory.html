Watching ShEPhERD-2 generate a molecule

ShEPhERD-2 · bioisosteric fragment merging · interactive

ShEPhERD-2 doesn't generate a molecule directly. It generates a molecule and the interaction profile that molecule presents to a protein — its electrostatic surface and its pharmacophores — jointly, in one reverse-diffusion process. Below is one of those trajectories, precomputed and played back in the browser.

This particular run is a bioisosteric fragment merge: the model was asked for a single molecule that reproduces the combined interaction profile of two separate crystallographic fragments. Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom, scrub the slider to step through the denoising, and toggle the three channels to see them independently.

360 denoising steps, shown every third step. Atom count changes between frames because ShEPhERD-2 diffuses composition along with geometry — atoms resolve into and out of existence until the molecule settles. Colors match the ChimeraX rendering used in the paper.

What you're looking at

x1 is the molecule itself: atom positions and identities. Bonds here are drawn from interatomic distances, not predicted by the model, which is why they flicker early in the trajectory and only lock in once the geometry is chemically sensible.

x3 is the electrostatic potential sampled on a shell around the molecule. Blue is positive, red negative. Early on it's noise; by the end it traces the molecule's actual charge distribution.

x4 is the pharmacophore set: typed interaction points with direction vectors where direction matters (hydrogen-bond donors and acceptors, aromatic ring normals). This is the channel that carries most of the conditioning signal — it's what lets you ask for a molecule that makes a specific set of contacts without specifying the molecule.
