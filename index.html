About Me

Hi! I'm a PhD candidate at MIT in Chemical Engineering and Computation, advised by Connor W. Coley. I build generative models for 3D molecular design that reason about how molecules interact — through shape, electrostatics, and pharmacophores — rather than only how they look as graphs.

Most of my work lives in the ShEPhERD family. ShEPhERD is an SE(3)-equivariant diffusion model that generates molecules matching a target interaction profile; ShEPhERD-2 turns that profile into a controllable design language, with pharmacophore prioritization, substructure constraints, and composition of multiple profiles; and ShEPhERD-score is the open-source toolkit that makes interaction profiles extractable, alignable, and differentiably scorable.

I also like taking generative design all the way to the bench — most recently in a campaign for the μ-opioid receptor with collaborators at UCSF, where a synthesis-constrained generative model matched the hit rates of a 3.8-billion-compound docking screen from a thousandth as many molecules. Before MIT I worked on first-principles thermochemistry for PFAS with Franklin Goldsmith at Brown, which produced autoCBH.

Outside of research I run, and I spend a good amount of time mentoring students finding their way into computational science. If any of this sounds interesting, feel free to reach out by email.

Featured Work

A molecule's interaction profile — its shape, electrostatic surface, and directional pharmacophores — is a practical, universal language for designing new molecules. ShEPhERD is what that idea looks like in practice.

ShEPhERD-2

Interaction profiles as a universal language for generative molecular design

Samples diverse molecules conditioned on a target interaction profile, with precise control through pharmacophore prioritization, substructure constraints, and the composition of multiple profiles. That control is what makes bioisosteric fragment merging, dual-target design, selectivity engineering, and peptide-to-small-molecule modality hopping possible in one model.

ShEPhERD ICLR 2025 Oral

Diffusing shape, electrostatics, and pharmacophores for bioisosteric drug design

An SE(3)-equivariant diffusion model over the joint distribution of 3D molecular structures and their interaction profiles, so a generated molecule can reproduce how a reference behaves without copying what it looks like.

Paper arXiv Code

ShEPhERD-score

Fast, adaptable 3D alignment and scoring of molecular features

The measuring stick for the rest of the family: conformer generation, interaction-profile extraction, differentiable 3D similarity, and alignment in PyTorch and JAX, together with the evaluation suite used to benchmark 3D generative models.

Other Projects

  • autoCBH

    Fragments molecular graphs into structure-conserving hierarchies and couples multi-fidelity quantum chemistry with experimental data through error cancellation, giving accurate heats of formation for molecules too large to compute directly. The subject of my master's thesis.

  • Generative screening for the μ-opioid receptor

    Docking, scoring, and compound downselection workflows around a synthesis-constrained generative model, carried through to experimental testing with collaborators at UCSF.

  • Cardiac safety modeling at Bristol Myers Squibb

    An ODE model of a human ventricular cardiomyocyte in MATLAB/Simulink, calibrated across a virtual population of cells to predict drug effects for cardiac toxicity screening.

  • Protein diffusiophoresis

    Non-equilibrium molecular dynamics studying how salt gradients drive protein transport, and what that means for stability and function. Ault Fluids Lab, Brown University.

  • Catalytic combustion reactors

    1D plug-flow and 2D rectangular reactor models for hydrogen–oxygen catalytic combustion.
