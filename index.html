Kento Abeywardane

PhD Candidate, Chemical Engineering & Computation, MIT · Coley Research Group

I build generative models for 3D molecular design that reason about how molecules interact — through shape, electrostatics, and pharmacophores — rather than only how they look as graphs. My work spans SE(3)-equivariant diffusion models, differentiable 3D similarity and alignment, and generative screening campaigns carried through to experimental validation.

Previously I earned an Sc.M. in Data-Enabled Computational Engineering & Sciences and an Sc.B. in Chemical Engineering at Brown University, where I worked on first-principles thermochemistry for PFAS in the Goldsmith Lab for Chemical Kinetics.

Featured research

ShEPhERD is a family of tools built around one idea: a molecule's interaction profile — its shape, electrostatic surface, and directional pharmacophores — is a practical, universal language for designing new molecules.

ShEPhERD-2

bioRxiv 2026

Interaction profiles as a universal language for generative molecular design

A 3D generative model that samples diverse molecules conditioned on a target interaction profile, with precise control through pharmacophore prioritization, substructure constraints, and the composition of multiple profiles. These enable bioisosteric fragment merging, dual-target design, selectivity engineering, and peptide-to-small-molecule modality hopping.

ShEPhERD

ICLR 2025 · Oral

Diffusing shape, electrostatics, and pharmacophores for bioisosteric drug design

An SE(3)-equivariant diffusion model that learns the joint distribution over 3D molecular structures and their interaction profiles, generating molecules in 3D that reproduce a reference's interactions. Selected for an oral presentation (top 1.8% of submissions).

ShEPhERD-score

Open source

Fast, adaptable 3D alignment and scoring of molecular features

A modular toolkit for conformer generation, interaction-profile extraction, differentiable 3D similarity scoring, and alignment in PyTorch and JAX — plus the evaluation suite used to benchmark 3D generative models.

Other projects

  • autoCBH

    Automates the fragmentation of molecular graphs into structure-conserving hierarchies, coupling multi-fidelity quantum chemistry with experimental data through error cancellation to predict heats of formation for large molecules. Basis of my master's thesis and the PFAS thermochemistry work.

  • Generative screening for the μ-opioid receptor

    Docking, scoring, and downselection workflows around a synthesis-constrained generative model (SynFormer), reaching experimental hit rates and affinities comparable to a concurrent 3.8B-compound docking campaign from only 1M generated samples. With collaborators at UCSF.

  • Cardiac safety modeling · Bristol Myers Squibb

    An ODE-based electrophysiological model of a human ventricular cardiomyocyte in MATLAB/Simulink, calibrated across a virtual population of cells to predict drug effects for cardiac toxicity screening.

  • Protein diffusiophoresis

    Non-equilibrium molecular dynamics (OpenMM) studying how salt gradients drive protein transport, and what that implies for stability and function. Ault Fluids Lab, Brown University.

  • Catalytic combustion reactors

    1D plug-flow and 2D rectangular reactor models for the catalytic combustion of hydrogen–oxygen systems, built with Cantera and COMSOL.

  • In-host viral load modeling

    A compartmental model of SARS-CoV-2 viral dynamics and the innate immune response, fit to clinical time-course data.
